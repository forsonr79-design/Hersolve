Real stories

Women who faced it first

Well-known women, the problems they faced, and what they actually did about them. Summaries are based on publicly reported accounts.

MONEY

Sara Blakely

Founder of Spanx

The problem
She wanted a smooth look under white trousers, but no pantyhose worked. She was selling fax machines and had very little spare money.
The story
In 2000 she started with about $5,000 in savings and no fashion background, making the first prototype and pitching it herself.
The solution
She cut the feet off a pair of pantyhose, refined the idea, and won over a Neiman Marcus buyer, which opened the door to the company's growth.
MONEY

J.K. Rowling

Author of Harry Potter

The problem
A single mother living on benefits in Edinburgh, she had a finished manuscript and almost no money.
The story
She wrote much of the first book in cafés while raising her daughter. Many publishers turned it down.
The solution
She kept submitting until Bloomsbury accepted it. It was published in 1997 and became the start of a worldwide series.
RELATIONSHIPS

Tina Turner

Singer and performer

The problem
In 1976 she left an abusive marriage with almost no money and debts to settle.
The story
She took whatever work she could, including cabaret and club shows, and rebuilt her career step by step.
The solution
She made the decision to leave and then rebuilt on her own. Her 1984 album Private Dancer, released in her mid-40s, became a major comeback.
BEAUTY

Rihanna

Founder of Fenty Beauty

The problem
Many foundation ranges offered few or no matches for deeper skin tones.
The story
Already a global star, she partnered with LVMH's Kendo incubator to build her own beauty brand.
The solution
Fenty Beauty launched in 2017 with 40 foundation shades, and the wider industry soon followed with bigger shade ranges.
CONFIDENCE

Oprah Winfrey

Media executive and talk-show host

The problem
In 1977 she was taken off the co-anchor desk at a Baltimore news station, reportedly for being too emotionally involved in stories.
The story
She moved to a local talk show, then to Chicago in 1984, and her national show launched in 1986.
The solution
Instead of copying the style she was criticised for lacking, she built on what set her apart: warmth and a conversational approach.
CONFIDENCE

Maya Angelou

Poet and author

The problem
After childhood trauma she barely spoke for roughly five years and struggled to find her voice.
The story
Books and poetry became her refuge. As a young adult she worked as a dancer, singer and cable car conductor while she kept writing.
The solution
She turned her experiences into writing: I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings (1969), and later a poem read at a 1993 U.S. presidential inauguration.
